How the scores, counts and “need” filters work

This is a transparent research-priority tool, not a financial forecast. There is no verified complete census of active local RMTs in this file. No listed MTAS practitioner does not mean no RMT. Only the mapped communities are covered; the map does not establish which unresearched communities have no services.

Counts: All 65 public directory pages were collected on November 12, 2026 using a fixed ordering seed. The snapshot contained 645 unique names, including entries outside Saskatchewan and entries with no public clinic address. Counts use an exact community token in an address ending in SK, ignoring case and whitespace. Community names are normalized for case, whitespace and punctuation. Each member is counted once per community even if they list multiple clinics there. Members can appear in more than one community. No address, nearby communities, mobile practitioners and other associations are not counted. Current activity and professional standing were not independently confirmed. MTAS explains the coverage limits.

“No listed RMT + need signal” requires a zero exact-community MTAS count and a positive Job Bank regional outlook (good or very good). La Loche additionally has a local published care-access study. Regional employment demand does not prove unmet local RMT demand. The stricter “Confirmed no RMT + local need” option has no matches: no community’s total absence of RMTs was verified. Nearby Air Ronge has one listed MTAS practitioner, so La Ronge should not be interpreted as an unserved area.

Research score: 0–100 weighted mean of housing affordability, regional outlook and documented room leads. Housing = 100 × (1,703 − city apartment rent) / 690, clamped to 0–100. The $1,013 and $1,703 endpoints are the lowest/highest of the seven comparable city rents used here, not the provincial minimum/maximum. Outlook = 100 for very good, 75 for good; an undetermined outlook is missing, not zero. Leads = 100 for a current price-disclosed rental advertisement, 80 for a dated current rent-based clinic lead with no price, 0 when none is verified. Zero means absence of verified evidence in this review, not absence of rentable rooms.

All three inputs are required for a score; missing housing or outlook yields “Not scored.” Lead evidence is still scored zero where no current lead was verified. Weights always total 100%. Balanced and room-lead views sort by unrounded score, then name. Lower living costs sorts by verified housing proxy, with unknown costs last. Cards display one decimal. Directory counts are deliberately excluded from scoring because association coverage is incomplete and uneven. Social media does not affect scores or filter eligibility.

Specializations: Saskatchewan counts match self-reported MTAS advanced education labels, deduplicated by person per community. Thai includes Thai yoga, hand/foot and table courses; these are not all equivalent qualifications. MLD includes MLD or lymph-drainage-labelled courses; lymphedema/decongestive training is shown separately. Prenatal/postnatal uses explicit pregnancy/pre-and-post-natal training labels. Sports uses explicit sports-massage labels. These do not establish current service availability or verified certification. Zero matching records is not zero providers. Newfoundland’s register has no comparable fields, so specialty counts are unknown rather than zero.

Costs: Apartment averages are CoStar’s November 2026 figures, not a standardized cost-of-living index. Unit size and listing mix vary. The budget calculator combines that housing proxy with your own assumptions; food, transport, utilities, tax, insurance and business overhead are not independently priced here. Northern communities are not assumed cheaper. No comprehensive comparison of housing purchase costs is included.

Need: Published regional occupational outlooks, local institutional context and local care-access research are distinct evidence types. Age, a hospital or physical work can suggest services to investigate, but cannot establish an RMT shortage. Confirm wait times, insurance/payer coverage, patient willingness to pay, room suitability and local relationships before leasing.

Map: Natural Earth’s generalized Saskatchewan polygon and GeoNames community points use the same spherical Lambert conformal conic projection, with standard parallels 49°N/60°N and central meridian 106°W. Population coverage is 2021 municipalities with at least 200 residents and matched GeoNames centres, including small towns and villages. Not all unincorporated settlements or reserves are included. Marker centres retain their projected geographic position; leader lines offset labels only. The full province is available with “ALL SK”; the initial view fits researched communities. Nearby distances use the haversine formula with Earth radius 6,371 km; they are straight-line distances, not routes or travel times. Marker sizes remain constant on screen; labels are hidden when they would collide. Use search to find every mapped community.

Sources, dates and social media disclosure

All sources were reviewed or retrieved November 12, 2026. Rental advertisements are leads to confirm, not guaranteed vacancies. Expired or inaccessible advertisements are labelled and excluded from current-lead filters and scores. Sources open in a new tab and require internet access.

Before choosing a location
  1. Call local clinics and check multiple professional association directories. Confirm active RMTs, mobile services, neighbouring communities and actual new-client wait times.
  2. Ask landlords or clinics for written rent, tax, utilities, laundry, accessibility, lease terms, zoning and insurance details. A job posting is not a lease offer.
  3. Validate patient interest, referral relationships and funding. For northern service models, work with community leadership and local health providers on priorities and culturally responsive care.
  4. Compare a short sublease or outreach pilot with a full lease, using realistic client volumes and your own costs.
