How the scores, counts and “need” filters work

This is a transparent research-priority tool, not a financial forecast. There is no verified complete census of active local RMTs in this file. No listed CMTNL practitioner does not mean no RMT. Only the mapped communities are covered; the map does not establish which unresearched communities have no services.

Scope: Island of Newfoundland and associated inhabited offshore municipalities. Labrador is excluded from the map. Job Bank’s west-coast economic region also includes Labrador; this is labelled. The source of each housing estimate is a CMA/CA, not the exact town. No rural town is assumed cheaper.

Counts: The CMTNL register snapshot has 655 rows, of which 605 have Active or dated Active status without a contradictory Inactive label. Misspelled and ambiguous statuses are excluded. Out-of-province records are not assigned to island towns. Registry IDs are deduplicated, then identical member names within each municipality are collapsed to avoid duplicate public entries. The audit list retains source records. Victoria, BC is excluded after an address check; the abbreviated city “Corner” is resolved to Corner Brook using the clinic page. City aliases are normalized: Mt. Pearl/Mount Pearl; CBS/Manuels/Conception Bay South; St. John’s spelling variants/Goulds; Grand Falls-Windsor spacing variants; Burin Bay Arm/Burin; Joe Batt’s Arm/Fogo Island; PCSP/Portugal Cove-St. Philip’s; Bell Island/Wabana; Newtown/New-Wes-Valley; Harbour Main/composite municipality. Obvious Carbonear and Spaniard’s Bay spelling errors are normalized. Ambiguous city names are not geocoded automatically. The College warns that active members can be missing. A zero is therefore not a verified absence of RMTs.

Need filters: “No listed RMT + need signal” means zero assigned listed-active registrants plus a positive regional outlook. In this dataset that signal applies only in Avalon. It is not proof of a local shortage. “Confirmed no RMT + local need” has no matches because no complete zero-RMT census was verified. Undetermined regional outlooks remain unknown.

Research score: 0–100 weighted mean of housing affordability, regional outlook and documented room leads. Housing = 100 × (1,361 − CMA/CA two-bedroom apartment rent) / 473, clamped to 0–100. The $888 and $1,361 endpoints are the lowest/highest of the four CMA/CA rents used here, not the provincial minimum/maximum. Outlook = 100 for very good, 75 for good; an undetermined outlook is missing, not zero. Leads = 100 for a current price-disclosed rental advertisement, 80 for a dated current rent-based clinic lead with no price, 0 when none is verified. Zero means absence of verified evidence in this review, not absence of rentable rooms.

All three inputs are required for a score; missing housing or outlook yields “Not scored.” Lead evidence is still scored zero where no current lead was verified. Weights always total 100%. Balanced and room-lead views sort by unrounded score, then name. Lower living costs sorts by verified housing proxy, with unknown costs last. Cards display one decimal. Registry counts are excluded from scoring because public entries can be missing, duplicated or outdated. Social media does not affect scores or filter eligibility.

Specializations: The public CMTNL register has no comparable specialization fields. Selected clinic profiles and the Lymphedema Association of Newfoundland and Labrador directory supply documented examples for MLD, prenatal and sports work. These are partial examples with source links, not a complete census, independent certification audit or confirmation of current service availability. Thai practitioner counts were not established. Unknown is never treated as zero. Specialty filters only match documented examples.

Costs: Apartment averages are CMHC’s October 2025 CMA/CA figures, not a standardized cost-of-living index. Unit size and listing mix vary. The budget calculator combines that housing proxy with your own assumptions; food, transport, utilities, tax, insurance and business overhead are not independently priced here. Northern communities are not assumed cheaper. No comprehensive comparison of housing purchase costs is included.

Need: Published regional occupational outlooks, local institutional context and local care-access research are distinct evidence types. Age, a hospital or physical work can suggest services to investigate, but cannot establish an RMT shortage. Confirm wait times, insurance/payer coverage, patient willingness to pay, room suitability and local relationships before leasing.

Map: Natural Earth’s detailed island-side provincial polygon and GeoNames community points use the same spherical Lambert conformal conic projection, with standard parallels 47°N/51°N and central meridian 56°W. Population coverage is 2021 municipalities with at least 200 residents and matched GeoNames centres, including small towns and villages. Not all unincorporated settlements or reserves are included. Marker centres retain their projected geographic position; leader lines offset labels only. The full province is available with “ISLAND”; the initial view fits researched communities. Nearby distances use the haversine formula with Earth radius 6,371 km; they are straight-line distances, not routes or travel times. Marker sizes remain constant on screen; labels are hidden when they would collide. Use search to find every mapped community.

Sources, dates and social media disclosure

All sources were reviewed or retrieved November 12, 2026. Rental advertisements are leads to confirm, not guaranteed vacancies. Expired or inaccessible advertisements are labelled and excluded from current-lead filters and scores. Sources open in a new tab and require internet access.

Before choosing a location
  1. Call local clinics and check multiple professional association directories. Confirm active RMTs, mobile services, neighbouring communities and actual new-client wait times.
  2. Ask landlords or clinics for written rent, tax, utilities, laundry, accessibility, lease terms, zoning and insurance details. A job posting is not a lease offer.
  3. Validate patient interest, referral relationships and funding. For northern service models, work with community leadership and local health providers on priorities and culturally responsive care.
  4. Compare a short sublease or outreach pilot with a full lease, using realistic client volumes and your own costs.
